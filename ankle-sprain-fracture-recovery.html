Ankle Sprain or Fracture?
How to Tell & Recover

Ankle injuries are among the most common reasons for an emergency visit. Knowing the warning signs avoids a missed fracture and a chronically unstable ankle.

Sprain vs Fracture

A sprain is a stretch or tear of the ligaments that hold the ankle together — most often the lateral ligaments on the outer side after the foot rolls inward. A fracture is a break in the bone: of the fibula or tibia (the “malleoli”), the talus or the base of the fifth metatarsal. Both cause pain, swelling and bruising, and they can coexist. About 15% of ankle injuries seen in emergency departments involve a fracture.

Sprains are graded: Grade 1 (stretched, minimal swelling), Grade 2 (partial tear, moderate swelling and bruising) and Grade 3 (complete tear, marked instability). A “high ankle sprain” injures the syndesmosis between the tibia and fibula and heals more slowly.

When Do You Need an X-ray? (Ottawa Ankle Rules)

Doctors use simple rules to decide who needs an X-ray. You need one if there is pain in the ankle or midfoot and any of:

  • Tenderness along the back edge or tip of the inner or outer ankle bone (lowest 6 cm)
  • Tenderness over the base of the fifth metatarsal or the navicular bone
  • Inability to take four steps both immediately after the injury and at the time of examination

These rules are very sensitive and reduce unnecessary X-rays, but they do not replace a clinician’s judgement.

First Aid: The First 72 Hours

  • Protect the ankle — use support or a brace and avoid forcing weight on it
  • Elevate above heart level when resting
  • Cold pack wrapped in cloth for 15–20 minutes every 2–3 hours
  • Compression with an elastic bandage, not so tight that toes turn blue or numb
  • Gentle ankle circles and alphabet-tracing as soon as pain allows — early movement helps ligaments heal
  • Avoid heat, alcohol and vigorous massage in the first 48–72 hours (they increase swelling)

Modern advice favours “POLICE” (protection, optimal loading, ice, compression, elevation) over complete rest: appropriate early loading speeds recovery.

Rehabilitation

1

Weeks 0–2: settle and move

Walk as pain allows with a brace; ankle range-of-motion exercises; calf and toe exercises.

2

Weeks 2–6: strengthen

Resistance-band exercises for the ankle in all directions, heel raises, single-leg standing.

3

Weeks 4–8: balance (proprioception)

Single-leg balance with eyes closed, wobble board, hopping drills. This is the part most people skip and is the best protection against repeat sprains.

4

Weeks 6–12: return to sport

Jogging, cutting and sport-specific drills when swelling is gone and strength and balance match the other side.

Most Grade 1–2 sprains recover in 2–6 weeks; Grade 3 sprains and high ankle sprains may need 8–12 weeks or more.

If It Is a Fracture

Stable fractures with the bones in position are treated with a cast or boot for about 6 weeks. Unstable fractures — where both sides of the ankle are broken (bimalleolar), the ankle is dislocated or the mortise is widened — are usually treated with surgery: plates and screws restore the ankle’s alignment so cartilage wears evenly. Weight-bearing is restricted for 6–12 weeks depending on the fracture and fixation, and physiotherapy restores movement afterwards. Diabetes, smoking and poor skin around the swollen ankle raise the risk of wound complications.

Chronic ankle instability: if the ankle keeps giving way after 3–6 months of good rehabilitation, a ligament reconstruction (such as a Bröstrom repair) is an effective operation for athletes and active people.

Go to Emergency If

  • The ankle looks deformed or the foot is pale, cold or numb
  • You cannot bear any weight at all
  • The skin is broken over a bump (open fracture)
  • Severe pain not relieved by elevation and medicine, tight swollen calf
  • Fever and redness after an injury or surgery

Common Questions

How long should I wear a brace?

For Grade 1–2 sprains, about 2–6 weeks during activity; many people use one during sport for a few months afterwards.

Is walking on a sprain harmful?

Walking as pain allows is helpful; limping on a painful ankle or forcing it is not.

Can I tell a fracture from a sprain at home?

Not reliably — use the Ottawa rules and get an X-ray if in doubt.

Do sprains lead to arthritis?

Repeated unrecovered sprains and chronic instability can, which is why rehabilitation matters.
