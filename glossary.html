Orthopaedic Glossary

92 bone, joint and spine terms explained simply — so you can follow what your doctor says.

Dr. Maninder SinghMS Orthopedics | Associate Professor, GMCH Amritsar
November 2026

Jump to: A · B · C · D · E · F · G · H · I · K · L · M · N · O · P · R · S · T · U · V · W · X  |  Conditions A–Z

A

Arthroplasty
An operation to replace or reshape a joint, such as hip or knee replacement.
Arthroscopy
Keyhole surgery in which a small camera and instruments are inserted into a joint through tiny cuts.
Avascular necrosis (AVN)
Death of bone because its blood supply is lost; commonly affects the femoral head and can lead to collapse of the joint.

B

Bursa / bursitis
A fluid-filled cushion between tendon and bone; bursitis is its inflammation.

C

Callus
New bone tissue that forms around a fracture as it heals.
Cartilage
The smooth, slippery tissue covering the ends of bones in a joint.
Cast
A rigid dressing, usually plaster or fibreglass, used to hold a fractured bone still while it heals.
Cauda equina syndrome
A surgical emergency in which the nerves at the bottom of the spinal canal are compressed, causing leg weakness, saddle numbness and bladder or bowel problems.
Cobb angle
The angle measured on an X-ray to describe the size of a spinal curve in scoliosis.
Compartment syndrome
Dangerous pressure build-up inside a muscle compartment, usually after a fracture or crush injury, that can cut off blood supply.
Contracture
Permanent shortening of muscle or soft tissue that limits joint movement.
Corticosteroid injection
An anti-inflammatory injection into a joint or around a tendon to relieve pain and swelling.
Crepitus
A crackling or grinding sensation or sound in a joint.
CT scan
An X-ray based scan that produces detailed cross-section images, especially good for bone.

D

Debridement
Surgical removal of dead, infected or damaged tissue.
Delayed union
A fracture that is healing slower than expected.
DEXA scan
A low-dose X-ray scan that measures bone mineral density to diagnose osteoporosis.
Disc (intervertebral disc)
A cushion between two vertebrae, with a tough outer ring and soft centre.
Dislocation
When the bones of a joint are completely displaced from their normal alignment.
Dupuytren’s contracture
Thickening of the palm tissue that gradually pulls fingers into a bent position.

E

Effusion
Excess fluid inside a joint, causing swelling.
EMG / nerve conduction study
Tests that measure electrical activity in muscles and nerves to diagnose nerve compression or injury.
Epicondylitis
Overuse tendon problem at the elbow — tennis elbow (outer) or golfer’s elbow (inner).
External fixator
A frame outside the body, attached to bone with pins, to hold a fracture stable.

F

Fasciitis
Inflammation or degeneration of a fascia, the tough connective tissue sheet — for example plantar fasciitis in the foot.
Femur
The thigh bone — the longest and strongest bone in the body.
Fixation
Using plates, screws, nails or wires to hold a broken bone in position while it heals.
Fracture
A break in a bone. May be closed (skin intact) or open (bone exposed).
Fusion (arthrodesis)
Surgery that joins two bones permanently, eliminating movement at that joint — commonly done in the spine.

G

Gait
The way a person walks.
Gout
A painful arthritis caused by uric acid crystals in a joint, classically the big toe.
Graft
Tissue (bone, tendon or synthetic) transplanted to repair or replace damaged tissue.
Greenstick fracture
A partial fracture in a child’s soft bone that bends like a green twig.

H

Haematoma
A collection of blood outside blood vessels, such as the clot that forms around a fracture.
Hemiarthroplasty
Replacing only one half (the ball) of a joint, commonly used for displaced hip fractures in older adults.
Herniated (prolapsed) disc
A disc whose soft centre pushes through the outer ring and may press on a nerve.
Hyaluronic acid injection
A gel-like injection into the knee sometimes used for osteoarthritis; benefits are modest.

I

Impingement
Pinching of soft tissue between bones as a joint moves, as in shoulder impingement.
Intramedullary nail
A metal rod placed inside the hollow centre of a long bone to fix a fracture.

K

Kyphosis
Forward rounding of the upper spine, often called a hunchback.

L

Lachman test
A hands-on examination used to check the ACL of the knee.
Laminectomy
Surgery that removes part of the vertebral bone (lamina) to relieve pressure on the spinal cord or nerves.
Ligament
A strong band of tissue that connects bone to bone and stabilises a joint.
Lordosis
The normal inward curve of the lower back and neck; excessive lordosis is called swayback.

M

Malunion
A fracture that heals in a poor position.
Meniscus
A C-shaped cartilage pad that cushions the knee.
Metatarsal
One of the five long bones in the middle of the foot.
Microdiscectomy
A small operation to remove the part of a disc pressing on a spinal nerve.
MRI
A scan using magnets and radio waves, the best test for soft tissues such as ligaments, tendons, discs and nerves.
Myelopathy
Dysfunction of the spinal cord, often from compression in the neck, causing clumsy hands and unsteady walking.

N

Non-union
A fracture that has failed to heal after an expected period, usually 6–9 months.
NSAID
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug such as ibuprofen or diclofenac.

O

Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF)
Surgery that exposes a fracture, realigns the pieces and fixes them with plates and screws.
Orthosis / brace
A device worn on the body to support, align or protect a joint or limb.
Osteoarthritis
Wear-and-tear arthritis in which joint cartilage thins and bone changes occur.
Osteomalacia
Softening of bone in adults, mostly from vitamin D deficiency.
Osteomyelitis
Bone infection.
Osteopenia
Bone density lower than normal but not yet low enough to be called osteoporosis.
Osteoporosis
A condition of weak, brittle bones with increased fracture risk.
Osteotomy
A surgical cut in a bone to realign it, for example to correct bow legs.

P

Patella
The kneecap.
Physiotherapy
Treatment using exercise, movement training and other techniques to restore function.
Plantar fascia
The thick band on the sole of the foot that supports the arch.
Ponseti method
The standard non-surgical treatment of clubfoot with weekly casts, a small tendon release and a brace.
Proprioception
The body’s sense of joint position and movement; balance training improves it.
PRP (platelet-rich plasma)
An injection prepared from a patient’s own blood to try to promote tendon or joint healing.

R

Radiculopathy
Pain, numbness or weakness caused by a pinched nerve root, such as sciatica.
Range of motion (ROM)
How far a joint can move in each direction.
Reduction
Putting a broken or dislocated bone back into position.
Rheumatoid arthritis
An autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the joints, causing swelling and stiffness.
Rickets
Soft, deformed bones in children from vitamin D, calcium or phosphate deficiency.
Rotator cuff
Four muscles and tendons that stabilise and move the shoulder.

S

Sciatica
Pain travelling from the lower back or buttock down the leg because of irritation of the sciatic nerve roots.
Scoliosis
A sideways curvature of the spine.
Spondylolisthesis
Forward slip of one vertebra over the one below it.
Sprain
A stretched or torn ligament.
Stenosis (spinal)
Narrowing of the spinal canal that squeezes the nerves, causing leg pain on walking.
Strain
A stretched or torn muscle or tendon.
Stress fracture
A tiny crack in a bone from repeated overload, common in athletes and military recruits.
Syndesmosis
The strong ligament joint between the tibia and fibula at the ankle.

T

Tendinopathy
A disorder of a tendon, usually from overuse and degeneration, causing pain and weakness.
Tendon
A tough cord connecting muscle to bone.
Tenotomy
Cutting or releasing a tendon, such as the heel cord release in clubfoot treatment.
Total knee / hip replacement
Operations that replace the damaged joint surfaces with metal and plastic implants.
Traction
A pulling force applied to a limb or the spine to align a fracture or relieve pressure.

U

Ultrasound
A scan using sound waves, useful for tendons, muscles and guiding injections.
Union
Healing of a fracture so that the bone ends are solidly joined.

V

Valgus / varus
Angle deformity: valgus points away from the midline (knock-knees), varus toward it (bow legs).
Vertebra
One of the bones that make up the spine.
Vitamin D
A vitamin that helps the body absorb calcium and keeps bones and muscles healthy.

W

Weight-bearing
Putting body weight through a limb, as in standing or walking.

X

X-ray
The most common imaging test for bones, using a small dose of radiation.

Definitions are simplified for general understanding. Educational use only — see our Terms & Medical Disclaimer.
