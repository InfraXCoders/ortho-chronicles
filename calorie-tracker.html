About the Calorie & Nutrition Tracker

Body weight is driven largely by energy balance — the calories you take in versus the calories you burn. This tracker helps you log everyday foods, with an emphasis on the Indian diet, and see your daily energy and macronutrient totals so you can make informed choices, whether the goal is losing fat, gaining muscle or simply eating more consistently.

Calories and macronutrients

Every food supplies energy from three macronutrients: protein, carbohydrate and fat. Protein is especially important for building and repairing muscle and for recovery after injury or surgery; carbohydrates are the body’s main fuel; and fats support hormones and the absorption of certain vitamins. Tracking these totals — not just calories — gives a much clearer picture of diet quality.

Nutrition for bone and muscle health

Because OrthoChronicles is an orthopaedic platform, it is worth highlighting the nutrients that keep the musculoskeletal system strong. Adequate protein, calcium and vitamin D are essential for bone density and fracture healing, and they become even more important with age. A diet persistently low in these raises the risk of osteoporosis and slows recovery. Pairing good nutrition with regular strength training is one of the best things you can do for lifelong bone health.

How to use it well

  • Log foods honestly and consistently for a week to see your true baseline.
  • Aim for a protein source at each meal, plenty of vegetables, and calcium-rich foods such as dairy, ragi or leafy greens.
  • Make gradual, sustainable changes rather than extreme restriction.

This tool provides estimates for general education and is not a substitute for advice from a registered dietitian, particularly if you have diabetes, kidney disease or another medical condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories do I need each day?

It varies with your age, sex, weight, height and activity level. The tracker estimates your maintenance needs and then adjusts for your goal — a modest deficit to lose weight or a small surplus to gain. As a rough guide many adults fall between 1,800 and 2,600 kcal a day, but your own calculated figure is what matters.

How is my calorie and macro target calculated?

The tool computes your basal metabolic rate using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, multiplies it by an activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure, and then splits your target into protein, carbohydrate and fat. Protein is set relative to your body weight to help preserve muscle.

Are the Indian food values accurate?

The database uses standard published values per 100 g for common Indian foods, fruits, vegetables and dishes. Home portions vary from one kitchen to another, so treat the numbers as a reliable guide rather than a laboratory measurement.

Is my data private, and is this medical advice?

Your log is stored locally in your own browser and only synced if you choose to sign in. It is a personal tracking aid, not medical or dietary prescription — anyone with a condition such as diabetes, kidney disease or an eating disorder should follow a plan set by their doctor or a registered dietitian.
