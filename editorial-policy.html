Editorial Policy

How we research, write, supervise and correct the health information on OrthoChronicles.

Why this policy exists

Health information can change decisions about surgery, medication and recovery, so it has to be accurate, understandable and honest about its limits. This page explains the standards behind every article, case study and tool on OrthoChronicles.info, so that readers — and the search and advertising platforms that list us — can see exactly how content is produced.

Who is responsible for the content

Clinical content is written or curated under the guidance of Dr. Maninder Singh, MS Orthopaedics and Associate Professor in the Department of Orthopaedics at Government Medical College & Hospital (GMCH), Amritsar. The platform itself is developed and maintained by Dr. Pawanpreet Singh. Every article carries the supervising doctor’s name, and the contact details for corrections are on our contact page.

How an article is produced

  • Topic selection. We write about conditions and operations that patients and trainees genuinely ask about — for example knee replacement, osteoporosis, low back pain and fracture healing — with particular attention to the Indian context, where care pathways, costs and risk factors differ from Western textbooks.
  • Drafting from clinical experience and recognised guidance. Articles are based on established orthopaedic principles, current clinical practice, and guidance from bodies such as the World Health Organization, NICE, the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons and specialty societies. Each article lists further reading so you can check the primary sources yourself.
  • Plain-language editing. We explain medical terms, use headings and summaries, and avoid exaggerated claims or guaranteed outcomes. Where evidence is uncertain we say so.
  • Medical supervision. The supervising orthopaedic surgeon reviews clinical statements before and after publication, particularly numbers, risks and recovery timelines.
  • Case studies. Our case library describes real clinical situations for education. Identifying details are removed and cases are presented as learning examples, not as promises of any particular result.

Keeping content current and correcting errors

Each article shows when it was last updated. We revisit pages when guidance changes or when readers point out problems. If you find something wrong, unclear or out of date, please tell us — we investigate every report and correct confirmed errors promptly, adjusting the “last updated” date when we do.

Independence and advertising

OrthoChronicles is free to read. Some pages may show advertising to cover hosting and development costs. Advertisers have no influence over what we write, how we rank topics or which treatments we discuss. We do not sell medicines or implants, we do not accept payment to feature products inside articles, and advertisements are kept visually separate from educational content. Any sponsored or affiliate relationship, if introduced in future, will be clearly labelled.

What we do not do

  • We do not diagnose conditions or recommend individual treatment online.
  • We do not publish anonymous or unreviewed clinical content.
  • We do not copy text from other websites; articles are written for this platform.

Interactive tools

Learning tools such as the 3D anatomy explorer, the DICOM scan reader, the radiology practice studio and the Calorie & Nutrition Tracker are educational aids. Calorie and nutrient values are typical estimates, and none of these tools replaces professional assessment.
