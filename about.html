About OrthoChronicles

A free orthopaedic education platform — surgeon-reviewed articles, real surgical case studies and interactive anatomy and radiology tools for patients, students and clinicians.

Our mission

OrthoChronicles was created to make trustworthy orthopaedic knowledge easy to find and easy to understand. Bone, joint and spine problems affect almost everyone at some point — through injury, ageing or sport — yet reliable, plain-language information written by people who actually treat these conditions can be hard to come by. Our goal is to bridge that gap for three audiences at once: patients trying to make sense of a diagnosis or an operation, students and residents learning musculoskeletal medicine, and practising clinicians who want a quick, well-organised reference.

What we offer

  • In-depth articles on common conditions and procedures — from knee replacement and hip replacement recovery to low back pain, osteoporosis, how fractures heal and managing road-accident injuries.
  • A real surgical case library spanning trauma, joint replacement, spine, paediatric and sports and hand surgery, with radiographs and outcomes.
  • Interactive learning tools: a 3D human anatomy explorer, a radiographic anatomy atlas, a DICOM scan reader, a radiology practice studio, and physiotherapy and gym exercise libraries.

Medical oversight & editorial standards

Because health information carries real consequences, every clinical article and case on OrthoChronicles is written or reviewed under the guidance of a qualified orthopaedic surgeon. Content is based on established orthopaedic principles and current clinical practice, written in clear language, and dated so readers know how recent it is. We revise material as guidance evolves. We do not sell treatments, we do not offer individual diagnoses online, and we clearly separate education from advertising.

Our commitment to accuracy — and its limits

We work hard to keep our content correct and up to date, but medicine is individual. Nothing on OrthoChronicles is a substitute for an in-person consultation, examination and investigations by your own doctor. Information here is for general education only; always seek personalised advice from a qualified professional before making decisions about your health. Our full terms are set out in the Terms of Use & Medical Disclaimer, and how we handle data is described in our Privacy Policy.

How the platform is funded

OrthoChronicles is free to use. To keep it that way and cover hosting and development, some pages carry advertising. Advertising never influences our clinical content, and ads are kept clearly distinct from the educational material on the page.
