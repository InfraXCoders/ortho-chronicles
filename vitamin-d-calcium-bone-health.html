Vitamin D, Calcium &
Strong Bones

Most Indians are short of vitamin D despite plentiful sunshine. Here is how to protect your bones at every age — without megadoses.

Why These Two Nutrients Matter

Bone is a living tissue that is constantly remodelled. Calcium is its main building material and vitamin D allows the gut to absorb calcium and helps muscle function. Deficiency in children causes rickets (soft, bowed bones); in adults it causes osteomalacia (bone pain, muscle weakness) and accelerates osteoporosis and fractures. Surveys across India find vitamin D deficiency in a majority of adults, driven by indoor lifestyles, clothing, air pollution, darker skin pigmentation and diets low in fortified foods.

How Much Do You Need?

Indicative daily needs (adult guidance from major bodies; your doctor may adjust):

Calcium (from food + supplements)
  • Children 4–8 years: about 800 mg
  • Teenagers: about 1,300 mg
  • Adults up to 50: about 1,000 mg
  • Women over 50 and everyone over 70: about 1,200 mg
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: about 1,000–1,200 mg
Vitamin D
  • Most adults: 600–800 IU (15–20 µg) a day from all sources
  • Older adults and those with proven deficiency may need more, under medical guidance
  • Upper safe limit about 4,000 IU/day without supervision
  • Check a 25-OH vitamin D blood level before long-term high doses

Many people overshoot by taking high-dose calcium tablets on top of a calcium-rich diet; food first is safer.

Food and Sunshine

  • Calcium-rich foods: milk, curd, paneer, cheese; ragi (finger millet); sesame (til); green leafy vegetables such as methi and sarson; almonds; small fish eaten with bones; fortified foods
  • Vitamin D: sunlight is the main source — about 15–30 minutes of midday sun on face, arms and legs a few times a week (skin colour, season and pollution affect this); oily fish, egg yolk, fortified milk and oils contribute small amounts
  • Limit very salty foods, excess caffeine and fizzy drinks, which can raise calcium loss
  • Eat enough protein; weight-bearing exercise and resistance training build bone density
Sun safety: avoid sunburn. Short regular exposure is enough; supplements are an alternative if you cannot get sun.

Who Should Get Tested?

  • People with bone or muscle pain, frequent fractures, or osteoporosis
  • Those with chronic kidney, liver or gut disease (including coeliac disease), or on long-term steroids, anti-epileptics or some HIV medicines
  • Housebound, elderly, veiled or very low-sun-exposure individuals
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women with risk factors, and exclusively breastfed infants (who need 400 IU/day as advised by the paediatrician)

A blood level below 20 ng/mL is generally considered deficient and 20–30 ng/mL insufficient; treatment is usually a course of higher-dose vitamin D followed by maintenance, guided by your doctor.

Supplements: Benefits and Cautions

  • Vitamin D tablets/sachets are safe at recommended doses; weekly or monthly dosing is common and effective
  • Calcium carbonate is best absorbed with food; calcium citrate can be taken any time; take no more than about 500 mg at one time
  • Very high doses of vitamin D or calcium can cause high blood calcium, kidney stones and heart-rhythm problems
  • People with kidney stones, kidney disease or parathyroid disease must ask a doctor first
  • Supplements do not replace osteoporosis medicines when they are indicated

Beyond Vitamins: Other Bone Protectors

  • Regular weight-bearing exercise (walking, stair climbing, dancing) and strength training
  • No smoking; limited alcohol
  • Maintain a healthy weight; very low body weight and crash diets weaken bone
  • Review long-term medicines such as steroids with your doctor
  • After 50 (women) or 65 (men) discuss a DEXA bone density scan
  • See our guide on osteoporosis

Common Questions

Is my daily 20 minutes of sunshine enough?

It depends on skin tone, season, time of day and how much skin is exposed. Many people still test low, so a blood test is useful.

Can I take calcium and vitamin D together?

Yes — vitamin D helps absorb calcium and they are often combined.

Does vitamin D prevent COVID or cancer?

Evidence for those claims is not strong. Its proven role is bone and muscle health.

Are milk and curd enough for calcium?

Two to three servings of dairy a day plus other calcium-rich foods usually cover needs.
